First, what is Qubigo?

A physical board game

  • A clear 3×3×3 cube you hold in your hands.
  • 27 stones, three properties each.
  • Three in a row, in any direction. 2–3 players, 20–30 minutes.
  • Easy to learn, hard to master.
Make sure people understand it is a physical game, in a box, that you can buy (qubigo.com). Photo from the Qubigo website. (Idea: add a photo of Anton and me here.)

The digital edition: an on-ramp

  • My capstone target. A real product, real releases, a real deadline.

Now with a design walkthrough: I'm in the loop

Source: docs/careful-plan-process.md and docs/assets/careful-plan-process.dot (current version). The design walkthrough machine writes a discussion file (journeys, events, policies, who owns what, questions about new guarantees or dependencies) and returns blocked until I have discussed and recorded decisions, one question at a time, with the discussion skill. A rerun starts again at the start; recorded decisions are kept. The planner can send the line back to the walkthrough when the next capability needs a new design choice; reviewers and the validator can block for the same reason. Routine tasks within the decisions go straight to the doer. This is the jidoka move: the line stops for a human decision.

What I take away

1Keep looking

We still need to look at the resulting design: the code, or any other probe.

2Slow

Planning with three brains and a design walkthrough feels slow.

3Contract

Sometimes the machines do not obey the contract.

Conclusions so far, in my words. 1: green checks and passing tests were not enough; the design in the code needed reading (genchi genbutsu again). 2: three reviewers that must all agree plus the walkthrough add a lot of waiting before any code. 3: for example a doer answer without the JSON result, or fenced, multi-line JSON; we added one tool-free repair for the missing result and still need independent validation.
